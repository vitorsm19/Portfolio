# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

European product teams, engineering managers, and founders evaluating a senior
frontend contractor for a defined engagement (3+ months). They land here from a
LinkedIn profile, a referral, or a search, usually on the clock, scanning for
proof: can this person ship production frontend, slot into our team, and own
delivery? Secondary readers: recruiters and agencies sourcing React/Next/TS
talent for client teams.

## Product Purpose

A single-page editorial portfolio that positions Vitor Mesquita as a **senior
frontend contractor** (React / Next.js / TypeScript, remote EU). Success = a
qualified inbound contract enquiry. The site itself is the proof of craft — the
design IS the pitch. It must read as the work of someone you'd trust with your
production frontend.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, editorial. Senior-engineer-who-also-has-taste. Voice is
direct and unhedged ("On time, built to scale, no excuses") — never salesy,
never junior-eager. Three words: **assured, crafted, exacting.** Emotional goal:
the visitor feels they've found a safe pair of hands with a point of view.

## Anti-references

- **Cheap web-designer / "I build websites for €500" energy.** Never. He is a
  senior product-frontend contractor for serious teams, not a template shop.
- Generic SaaS-landing scaffolding: eyebrow-on-every-section, identical icon-card
  grids, hero-metric template, gradient text.
- Costume "developer" aesthetics: monospace sprayed on everything to signal
  "technical." He is technical; he doesn't need to cosplay it.
- Cluttered freelancer-marketplace pages (Fiverr/Upwork vibe).

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the portfolio.** Every detail is evidence of craft; a sloppy
   transition is a lost contract.
2. **Show seniority through restraint, not volume.** One confident statement
   beats three repetitive pitch sections. Concise reads as senior.
3. **Editorial confidence.** Oversized display typography and deliberate negative
   space carry the page; decoration earns its place or is cut.
4. **Honest qualification over hype.** Tell the reader plainly who this is and
   isn't for — qualifying out is more senior than chasing every lead.
5. **Motion as polish, not spectacle.** Movement proves attention to detail; it
   never gets in the way of reading.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA target. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 against its surface in both themes.
Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support (already wired). Dark + light themes,
both first-class. Keyboard-reachable nav and controls; semantic landmarks.
